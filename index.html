Leave1%

My first app

As AI coding tools became easier to use, I decided to build something myself. This app was designed to help people recognize obsessive-compulsive patterns, and AI wrote all of its code.

It worked. To me, though, it was a black box: I couldn't fix it, and I couldn't tell whether it was safe.

I realized that I wanted to work with AI, not simply depend on it.

An early prototype, shown as it is. It is not a clinical tool.

Where Should AI Stop?

Where AI should hand decisions back to people, from video editing to care

Leave1% left me with a black box. I had spent years behind a camera and in the edit, so I knew how much judgment sits inside a single cut. I asked: as AI takes on more of the work, which decisions do people still want to keep? I synthesized public creator discussions, industry framing and tool examples to find out.

  • People hand off repetitive execution but keep pacing, voice, story, style and the final call.
  • Four conditions move that boundary: stakes, editability, intent and authority. In mental health care, they matter even more.
  • It also traces my own work, from handing everything to AI to staying in the loop.

“AI removes decisions until a decision becomes worth making.”

Into the Stars

A game about sleep, in development

Most sleep products treat sleep as a problem to fix. I wanted to try the opposite: what if sleep itself could be the content, something to play with and enjoy rather than a task to complete? Into the Stars is my attempt to turn sleep into an entertainment IP.

For the past three months I have worked on its engineering almost every day. I direct the design, review AI-generated plans and code, and build and test it myself in Godot. When AI couldn't fix a bug in the HUD's architecture, I restructured it myself, placing the three top-screen elements at the same level of the scene tree.

The process thrilled me, and it showed me how much technical depth I still need to bring more complex ideas to life.

A lightweight playable version is in progress.

MoodOwn

Three proposed AI-native products and a two-year plan

I brought that principle back to mental health. AI can meet people where they are and help them think, but at the moments that matter it should stop and connect them with the right person. MoodOwn proposes three products, for families, for couples and for healthcare workers.

  • Fifteen interactive concept screens, with private spaces by default and sharing item by item.
  • A shared agent architecture that defines where AI stops and hands over to a person.
  • An honest map of what I can do today, what I still need, and how I plan to learn it over two years.

Mood down or MoodOwn. Let's find the way together.
